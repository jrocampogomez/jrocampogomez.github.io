About

Stanford · KIPAC

I’m a physics major at Stanford University, where I work with Professor Vahé Petrosian on AGN luminosity evolution and Professor Steven Allen on reverberation mapping, through the Kavli Institute for Particle Astrophysics and Cosmology (KIPAC).

I started at Los Angeles City College, did my first research there through the NASA California Space Grant Consortium under the supervision of Prof. Jayesh Bhakta, and transferred to Stanford as a Jack Kent Cooke Undergraduate Transfer Scholar. It’s not the path I expected to take, but it’s the one that brought me here.

Outside of astrophysics, I’m a co-lead on the Policy Team at the Stanford Student Space Initiative, where I work on international space law, space security, and nuclear nonproliferation. I’m also involved in STEM outreach, from mentoring community college students through Stanford’s Community College Outreach Program to facilitating public observing nights, where I operate telescopes and help visitors explore the night sky. A big part of what I care about is making science feel accessible—whether that means helping someone navigate a path into STEM or simply giving them a chance to look through a telescope and ask questions.

Long term, I want to work in astrophysics, wherever that takes me between theory and observation, while staying involved in the broader questions surrounding science, technology, and society. Outside of physics, I write poetry and fiction, and I like spending time outdoors, whether that's under a clear night sky, at the beach, or somewhere out in the woods.

Cosmological evolution of FSRQ luminosity functionsActive

KIPAC, Stanford University  ·  Advisor: Prof. Vahé Petrosian  ·  June 2025–present
Methods: Efron–Petrosian permutation test · Lynden-Bell C− · K-corrections · bootstrap

A flat-spectrum radio quasar makes it into this sample only if it is bright enough in γ-rays, bright enough in radio, and has a measured optical redshift — three separate truncations acting on one dataset. I’m studying the cosmological evolution of the γ-ray and radio luminosity functions of FSRQs using a tri-variate sample built from Fermi-LAT, VLBI, and Gaia DR2 observations, accounting for the additional selection bias introduced by optical redshift measurement.

Using the Efron–Petrosian method and Lynden-Bell’s C− estimator, I’m recovering the luminosity evolution and local luminosity functions of the population. Once the evolution is removed, the same analysis allows me to measure the intrinsic γ-ray–radio luminosity correlation and investigate the relationship between jet and accretion-disk emission.

The Fe II emitting region in AGNActive

KIPAC, Stanford University  ·  Advisor: Prof. Steven Allen  ·  Mentor: Dr. Zhefu Yu  ·  June 2025–present
Methods: PyCCF · JAVELIN · PyROA · simulation-based validation

Reverberation mapping converts a time delay into a physical size, so a radius or a black hole mass is only as trustworthy as the lag it came from. I’m measuring reverberation lags between the AGN continuum and UV Fe II emission across 25 OzDES-monitored active galactic nuclei at z ≈ 1–2, using multi-year photometric and spectroscopic observations.

I’m applying and cross-validating three independent lag estimators, then using simulations to quantify their accuracy and failure modes under realistic noise and sampling. The goal is to constrain the Fe II radius–luminosity relation and ultimately use it to estimate supermassive black hole masses.

Space law, space security, and a rocketry framework for UruguayActive

Stanford Student Space Initiative  ·  Policy researcher, Sept 2025–present  ·  Policy Team co-lead since March 2026
Areas: international space law · space security · nuclear nonproliferation · satellite traffic management

Amateur and high-power rocketry in Uruguay sits outside any dedicated national licensing or airspace regime. Under Article VI of the Outer Space Treaty, however, a state is responsible for the activities of its non-governmental actors whether or not it has authorised them. I’m working on a project to draft a model regulatory framework for the activity, comparing existing regimes such as the FAA’s amateur rocket classifications and the safety codes developed by the hobby itself, and considering which licensing, motor-classification, and airspace-coordination provisions could transfer into Uruguayan civil aviation law.

The larger goal is to explore whether Uruguay could become a regional hub for amateur rocketry rather than simply a place where the activity is permitted. That raises questions about launch-site siting, third-party liability, and how a small state can build regulatory capacity where a dedicated framework does not yet exist. I also co-authored a policy paper on preventing the deployment of nuclear weapons in orbit and lead additional work on satellite traffic management.

I like that the work sits somewhere between technical research and practical policy: the questions are grounded in science and engineering, but the answers have to work in the real world.

Flight telemetry for experimental radio-controlled aircraftCompleted

NASA California Space Grant Consortium, Los Angeles City College  ·  Advisor: Prof. Jayesh Bhakta  ·  April 2023–April 2024
Technologies: Raspberry Pi Pico · MicroPython · nRF24L01 · Java / Processing

I built a low-cost flight telemetry and data-logging system for experimental RC aircraft, integrating inertial, barometric, GPS, and current sensors on a Raspberry Pi Pico programmed in MicroPython. The system used a packet-based nRF24L01 radio link alongside onboard microSD logging, so data would be preserved even if the radio connection was lost.

I also developed a real-time ground-station display in Java and Processing, deriving aircraft pitch and roll from three-axis accelerometer data. During flight testing, I identified radio-frequency interference between the aircraft’s control and telemetry transmissions as the source of instability in the downlink.

Repository and poster · presented at SCCUR 2023 and NCUR 2024

A lot of my research happens computationally, so this is where I keep some of the analysis and simulation work behind the projects above.

FSRQ luminosity evolution analysis

Python · NumPy · SciPy · Astropy · pandas · Jupyter

Computational analysis of a tri-variate FSRQ sample constructed from Fermi-LAT γ-ray, VLBI radio, and Gaia DR2 optical observations. The analysis includes sample construction and truncation limits, K-corrections, the Efron–Petrosian method, and Lynden-Bell’s C− estimator to recover the luminosity evolution, differential luminosity functions, cumulative density functions, and co-moving density evolution of the population.

I’m also characterizing the resulting luminosity functions with broken-power-law models and estimating uncertainties through bootstrap resampling. Once the evolution is removed, the same analysis can be used to measure the intrinsic γ-ray–radio luminosity correlation and investigate the relationship between jet and accretion-disk emission.

Lag-recovery testing for reverberation mapping

Python · PyCCF · JAVELIN · PyROA · simulated light curves

Computational tools for measuring and validating reverberation lags in the OzDES data described above. I run three independent lag estimators over the same light curves and use simulations with realistic survey cadence and noise to test how reliably each method recovers known input lags.

A current focus is developing quality cuts that distinguish reliable lag measurements from cases where the recovered lag is sensitive to noise, sampling, or other features of the light curves. These cuts will be used to establish a reliable sample for constraining the Fe II radius–luminosity relation and, ultimately, black hole masses.

Bayesian AGN SED modelling

Python · custom Metropolis–Hastings MCMC · custom numerical integration  ·  Stanford Physics 113, 2026

A Python implementation of thin-disk spectral energy distribution fitting for AGN, using my own Metropolis–Hastings MCMC implementation and numerical integrator to infer black hole masses and accretion rates from observational data. I reproduced published measurements for high-redshift blazars and tested how the inferred parameters depend on the numerical integration scheme.

RC aircraft telemetry firmware and ground station

MicroPython · Raspberry Pi Pico · Java / Processing  ·  github.com/jrocampogomez/RC-Plane-Telemetry…

Onboard firmware reading an IMU, barometer, GPS, and current sensor over I²C and SPI, writing to microSD and transmitting over a packet radio link; plus a ground-station application that decodes the stream and plots orientation and power telemetry live. Public repository and poster.
